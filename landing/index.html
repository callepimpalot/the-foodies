How it works

The loop

Capture → Plan → Shop → Cook. All four stages are built and live.

01

Capture

Paste a recipe as text, attach a photo or screenshot, or both at once — or drop in a link from a recipe blog or a social post and let it read the caption. It comes back as a structured recipe, and before anything saves you can ask for changes until it's right.

Text + photo + link
02

Plan

One meal a day, laid out a few days at a time. Pick a saved recipe, mark a night as leftovers, or leave yourself a note. There is also a planning chat that curates a shortlist with you instead of guessing at a whole week.

Manual or curate-with-chat
03

Shop

Once the plan is locked, the ingredients from every recipe on it get pulled together into one checkable list, consolidated, sorted and ready to hand to whoever is in the shop.

04

Cook

A step-by-step view for when your hands are actually busy — a servings stepper that scales quantities live, and a countdown timer built in, so you're not switching apps mid-recipe.

Worth saying plainly

What it isn't

—No ads, ever

Nothing trying to sell you a different recipe than the one you saved.

—No public catalog

What's in it is what your household cooked — not a feed to scroll, not a browsable market.

—No strangers in your kitchen

Other families can't see your recipes, your plan or your list. That separation is enforced by the database, not by good manners.

—No growing pile of screenshots

Recipes stop living in your camera roll and your messages app, and start living somewhere both of you can find them.

Most recipe apps make their money on your attention or your card. This one is built to be used at 6pm on a Tuesday.

Want a household?

It's live and in daily use. Households are created by invite while the account system lands — leave an email and you'll get one.
